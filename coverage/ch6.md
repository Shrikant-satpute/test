# Ch 6 — Prevention of Oppression & Mismanagement (Sec 241–245)

## Must Cover
| Sec | Topic | Tricky Points |
|---|---|---|
| 241 | Application to Tribunal | "Oppression" = prejudicial to member/public; "Mismanagement" = prejudicial to co./public — different ground, same remedy |
| 242 | Tribunal powers | Regulate affairs; appoint directors/MD; alter AoA; buyback order; restrict transfer; **winding up** as a remedy |
| 243 | Effect of orders | Terminated agreement → NO compensation by co. |
| 244 | Eligibility | Share-capital co.: 100 OR 1/10 (**whichever LESS**); no-share-capital: 1/5 of total members; Tribunal **can waive** on application |
| 245 | Class action | Against co./directors/auditors/experts; 100 members OR 10% membership; **+ 5% issued share capital** as third alternative; **no Tribunal waiver** |

## Top Traps
- Sec 244 — **"LESS"** (students apply "more").
- No-share-capital threshold: **1/5** (not 1/10).
- Sec 244 waivable; Sec 245 **NOT** waivable.
- Class action may target **AUDITOR** — not just directors.
- Sec 242 can order winding-up as a remedy — no separate petition needed.
- Sec 243: director loses contract → no compensation from co.
- Sec 241 admissible even if applicant unharmed personally, if "public interest" affected.
- Sec 245 adds the 5% issued-capital alternative — three routes total.

## Hard Scenario Hooks
- No-share-capital trust, 200 members, 35 apply → meet 1/5 threshold? (No: need 40.)
- Tribunal waives 244 eligibility → can rival shareholder challenge?
- Class action against auditor signing manipulated accounts — proper respondent?
- NCLT orders winding-up as remedy → can dissenting shareholders appeal to NCLAT?
- Director's 5-yr contract terminated by Tribunal — ₹1.5 Cr claim valid?
- 0.5%-holder gets waiver; co. challenges the waiver order — appealable?
